# 0005 — Develop scopes through purpose-led dialogue

**Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Status:** Proposed (agreed in design discussion; pending human review of [PR #8](https://github.com/Motor-Learning-Lab/HILD/pull/8))

**Context:** In the first test of `hild-integrate` on [HILD-onboarding-test](https://github.com/opherdonchin/HILD-onboarding-test), the agent followed its read-only safety rules but overwhelmed the new researcher with technical inventory, a complete proposed scope, and an installation approval question. It did not first explain how HILD could benefit the researcher's work or teach scoping as a shared activity.

**Decision:** For researchers unfamiliar with HILD, the agent should first explain HILD's value for their project, then invite collaborative scope-building. Once invited, it should **develop** rather than immediately present a finished scope. The scope must ultimately capture **goal, boundaries, evidence and autonomy**, but these are dimensions of the agreement, **not a fixed question sequence or form**. Technical investigations remain rigorous; their presentation is staged around actual decisions. A single, explained default is preferable when it is sensible and reversible; compare alternatives when tradeoffs warrant it. When a blocker becomes relevant, work with the researcher to diagnose a feasible route. The [authoritative HILD process](../process.md) defines general scoping principles. The [onboarding companion](../../.agents/skills/hild-scope/onboarding-scope.md) is the **single procedural home for newcomer-specific introductions and first-scope examples**, referenced by both integration and scoping skills instead of duplicated in them.

**Why:** The researcher learns how HILD works through a conversation that develops shared understanding and agency. Presenting a fully formed technical contract before establishing purpose undermines HILD's educational and supervisory goals, even when the contract is accurate.

**Consequences:** The introductory procedure is shared across *pre-installation* integration and *post-installation* onboarding; the companion must be fetched from the same upstream revision when not yet installed. A request to **begin scoping** does not authorize creation of a scope issue, approval of a completed scope, or implementation. The completed GitHub issue remains detailed and reviewable. New first-contact experiments should check the researcher's comprehension and the quality of dialogue, as well as no-write safeguards; one good result is not proof of agent-wide compliance.

**Origin:** The human's onboarding-test feedback documented in [scope #4](https://github.com/Motor-Learning-Lab/HILD/issues/4#issuecomment-6234102150) and [clarification](https://github.com/Motor-Learning-Lab/HILD/issues/4#issuecomment-6234198923).
